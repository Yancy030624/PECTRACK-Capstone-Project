## Related Literature

Local Literature

Predictive Analytics

Predictive Analytics for Sales Forecasting and Inventory Management

Divina, Olan, and their colleagues (2025) developed a data-driven point-of-sale and inventory system for Pastil sa Tabi that combines sales forecasting with predictive

<!-- PDF page 58 -->

analytics. The study aimed to improve transaction processing, inventory monitoring, and demand prediction by integrating these functions into one system. Its approach demonstrates how historical sales records can help a food business estimate future demand and make better inventory decisions. However, forecasting accuracy depends on the quality of the available records and the sales patterns of the business. This study is relevant to PECTRACK because it provides a local example of integrating predictive analytics with sales and inventory management for a food-related enterprise.

Predictive Analytics and Linear Regression for Sales Forecasting

Lo, Tolentino, and their colleagues (2025) developed RELTRACK, a web-based system for inventory, appointments, and sales forecasting for REL Glass and Aluminum Works. The system uses linear regression to analyze sales data and estimate future sales performance. By combining forecasting with inventory monitoring, the system supports decisions based on recorded business transactions rather than relying entirely on manual estimates. However, its business setting differs from a bakery, where products may be perishable and daily demand may change considerably. This study is relevant to PECTRACK because it demonstrates how linear regression can be integrated into a web-based system to support sales forecasting and inventory planning.

Sales Forecasting for Inventory Control in Small Businesses

Orquesta (2026) examined the development of a computerized point-of-sale and inventory system with sales forecasting for micro, small, and medium enterprises in Pagadian City. The system incorporated several forecasting methods, including moving averages, linear regression, weighted moving averages, quadratic regression, and Holt-Winters seasonal smoothing. These methods can help businesses examine historical sales patterns and estimate future demand. However, the most suitable method depends on the availability of historical records and the characteristics of the products being sold.

<!-- PDF page 59 -->

This study is relevant to PECTRACK because it provides potential forecasting methods that can be evaluated using Pecto’s Bakery sales data.

Predictive Analytics in E-Commerce and Market Demand Estimation

Culili (2026) developed an e-commerce platform for the Department of Agrarian Reform Region 02's Agrarian Reform Beneficiary Organizations, incorporating predictive analytics for sales forecasting and market demand estimation. The study addressed challenges involving sales tracking, inventory management, and market reach. Its findings highlight how integrating online transactions with predictive analytics can support planning and data-driven decision-making. However, agricultural enterprises have different operational needs from a bakery that produces and sells fresh products daily. This study is relevant to PECTRACK because it demonstrates how predictive analytics can work alongside online ordering and inventory records to help a business anticipate demand.

Predictive Sales Analytics and Inventory Decision-Making

Custodio, Lucero, and their colleagues (2026) presented a predictive sales and inventory system that uses linear regression for sales forecasting and K-means clustering for customer segmentation. The system combines sales transactions, inventory monitoring, and customer information to support business decisions. However, its customer segmentation feature may not be necessary for every small business, and the usefulness of its predictions depends on the quality of the data collected. This study is relevant to PECTRACK because its sales forecasting component could help Pecto’s Bakery estimate demand for individual products and make more informed inventory decisions.

Foreign Literature

Predictive Analytics

Predictive Analytics for Retail Inventory and Demand Forecasting

<!-- PDF page 60 -->

Krishnamurthy, Nadukuru, and their colleagues (2024) discussed how predictive analytics can improve inventory management and demand forecasting in retail businesses. Their work examined approaches such as time-series analysis, machine learning, and causal forecasting to estimate future demand and support inventory decisions. The study also discussed challenges related to data quality and system integration. However, some analytical approaches may require technical resources that smaller businesses do not have. This literature is relevant to PECTRACK because it explains how historical sales data can support inventory planning and help reduce the risk of product shortages or excess stock.

Integrating Demand Forecasting with Inventory Control

Van der Haar and Wellens (2024), together with their co-authors, investigated how supervised learning can connect forecasting with inventory control. Rather than treating demand prediction and inventory decisions as completely separate activities, their approach directly estimates inventory-related decisions using available data. The study also considered situations involving perishable goods and lost sales. However, its methods may require sufficient historical data and careful adjustment to a business's operating conditions. This study is relevant to PECTRACK because bakery products have limited shelf lives, making it important to consider expected demand when planning stock and production quantities.

Machine Learning for Retail Sales Forecasting

Pattnaik, Padhi, and their colleagues (2025) examined artificial intelligence and machine learning approaches for forecasting sales using data from Big Bazaar. Their research compared different predictive models and grouped products into categories, including fresh and perishable foods, packaged foods, and household items. The study highlights the importance of evaluating different models to determine which provides more accurate forecasts for particular product categories. However, a large retail chain may have more extensive transaction records and resources than a small bakery. This study is relevant to

PECTRACK because it supports the use of product-level sales data to estimate future demand and improve production planning.

Automated Machine Learning for Retail Forecasting

The researchers of Comparative Study of AutoML and Machine Learning in Retail Forecasting (2025) compared traditional machine-learning models with automated machine-learning approaches using transaction data from an Indonesian traditional store. The study evaluated forecasting performance using several error measurements and found that model performance varied depending on the method and characteristics of the data. However, automated tools may still require data preparation and model evaluation to produce reliable results. This study is relevant to PECTRACK because it emphasizes the importance of selecting a forecasting method that balances accuracy with practical implementation requirements.

Predictive Analytics for Sales Forecasting and Inventory Optimization

Mansur, Sattar, and their colleagues (2025) investigated a hybrid neural-network approach for retail sales forecasting. Their model combined convolutional neural networks and long short-term memory networks while considering factors such as holidays, salary days, protests, and weather conditions. The study reported improved forecasting performance by considering both historical sales and relevant external factors. However, a complex neural-network model may not be necessary for a small bakery if simpler methods can produce sufficiently accurate predictions. This research is relevant to PECTRACK because it highlights how historical sales patterns and relevant calendar factors can help estimate future demand during weekends, holidays, and other periods when bakery sales may change.

Local Studies

A Web-Based Sales and Inventory System for Small Business Enterprises

Cruz, Mendoza, and Dela Cruz (2022) developed a web-based sales and inventory system for small business enterprises. The study aimed to improve transaction processing and record management by replacing manual operations with a digital platform. The results indicated that the system improved transaction accuracy and reduced processing time. It also helped businesses keep organized and accessible records, thus enhancing operational efficiency. However, the study mainly targeted general retail environments and did not include features specific to food-related businesses, such as order customization, product perishability, and ingredient inventory management.

An Online Ordering System with Integrated Inventory Management for Local Food Businesses

Lopez, Garcia, and Reyes (2023) created an online ordering system integrated with inventory management specifically for local food businesses. The study aimed to enhance both customer experience and internal operations by combining ordering and stock monitoring in one system. The findings revealed that the system boosted customer satisfaction and reduced order errors. Furthermore, the integration ensured consistency between sales and inventory levels, lowering discrepancies in stock records. Despite these advantages, the study lacked advanced tracking and reporting features, which are essential for monitoring business performance and making informed decisions.

Foreign Studies

Order Management Systems and Transaction Efficiency

Order Management Systems (OMS) play a significant role in improving the efficiency, accuracy, and coordination of business transactions. According to Alzoubi and Snider (2022), order management systems help businesses automate order entry, monitor transaction progress, and improve coordination between inventory, sales, and customer service operations. Their study explains that integrating OMS with centralized database systems allows businesses to manage customer orders systematically while reducing delays, data inconsistencies, and human error.

Furthermore, the study highlights that digital order management systems improve operational visibility by allowing businesses to monitor order status, inventory availability, and transaction records in real time. Businesses that implement OMS benefit from faster transaction processing, improved customer communication, and more accurate order handling. However, the study also notes that many existing order management systems are designed for medium and large-scale enterprises, making them costly and complex for small businesses with limited technical resources.

This study is relevant to the proposed system because it supports the need for a structured and automated Order Management System that improves transaction efficiency, customer order monitoring, and inventory coordination. It also reinforces the importance of developing a system specifically tailored to the operational needs of small businesses such as Pecto’s Bakery by focusing on usability, accessibility, and integration of essential functions including order management, inventory tracking, customer records, and payment monitoring.

Local Systems

Foodpanda(2024)

In the Philippines, digital platforms such as Foodpanda are widely used to enhance business operations through automation and online services. Foodpanda operates as an online food delivery platform that connects customers, partner merchants, and delivery riders through a centralized system. It utilizes a platform-based architecture supported by client-server architecture and cloud infrastructure, allowing users to access services via mobile applications or web interfaces. The system also applies concepts from e-commerce systems and service-oriented architecture (SOA), where services such as ordering, payment processing, and delivery tracking function as interconnected components. While Foodpanda provides convenience and faster transactions, it acts as a third-party intermediary, limiting business control over pricing, operations, and customer data.

GrabFood(2024)

GrabFood, another widely used platform in the Philippines, functions similarly as an online food delivery system. It follows a platform ecosystem model where multiple stakeholders—customers, merchants, and delivery personnel—interact within a unified system. GrabFood employs cloud-based client-server architecture and integrates real-time processing to manage orders, payments, and delivery tracking efficiently. It also applies principles of distributed systems, ensuring scalability and reliability across high-demand usage. However, like Foodpanda, GrabFood operates as a third-party platform, which restricts direct control of businesses over their operations, customer relationships, and

## Synthesis

The reviewed local literature shows that predictive analytics can help businesses understand past sales, identify patterns, and estimate future product demand. The studies of Divina and colleagues (2025), Lo and colleagues (2025), Orquesta (2026), Culili (2026), and Custodio and Lucero and colleagues (2026) discuss how data analysis can support business planning and decision-making. These findings are relevant to PECTRACK because Pecto’s Bakery can use its historical order and sales records to estimate which products may have higher demand. This can help the bakery plan its inventory and production based on available data rather than relying only on manual observation.

Foreign literature also highlights the value of predictive analytics in improving business operations. The works of Krishnamurthy and Nadukuru and colleagues (2024), van der Haar and Wellens and colleagues (2024), Pattnaik and Padhi and colleagues (2025), the AutoML study (2025), and Mansur and Sattar and colleagues (2025) discuss approaches to analyzing data and supporting predictions. These studies provide a basis for incorporating predictive analytics into PECTRACK. However, the system should use an approach that fits the bakery’s available data and operational needs. The predictions will serve as a guide for planning and will not guarantee actual sales or demand.

The reviewed local studies on web-based sales, inventory, and order management systems show the importance of organizing business transactions through a centralized system. The studies of Cruz, Mendoza, and Dela Cruz (2022), Lopez, Garcia, and Reyes (2023), Santos and Villanueva (2023), Taqueban and Villamor (2024), and Salazar (2024) support the use of digital tools to manage orders, monitor product availability, and organize business records. These findings are relevant to PECTRACK because Pecto’s Bakery currently handles orders and records through different methods, including Facebook messages, walk-in transactions, and handwritten documents. A web-based Order Management System can help organize these activities and make order information easier to access and monitor.

Foreign studies, including the work of Alzoubi and Snider (2022), further support the use of order management systems to improve coordination and organize order-related activities. These systems show the importance of managing order information throughout the transaction. For PECTRACK, this means providing a more organized way to record customer orders, monitor their status, and coordinate order preparation and fulfillment. By keeping order details in one system, the bakery may reduce problems caused by duplicate entries, unclear handwritten records, and missed order updates.

The reviewed local systems, such as Foodpanda, GrabFood, Jollibee, Red Ribbon, and Goldilocks, demonstrate how digital platforms can help customers access products and place orders. However, some of these platforms mainly focus on food delivery or are designed for larger businesses. Their features and operating processes may not fully match the specific needs of Pecto’s Bakery. This supports the need for a system designed around the bakery’s actual order management practices, including customer orders, product availability, order monitoring, and preparation for pickup or delivery.

Foreign systems such as Shopify, Square for Restaurants, StoreHub POS, Toast, Domino’s, and Uber Eats also demonstrate different ways of managing orders, sales, and inventory through digital tools.
