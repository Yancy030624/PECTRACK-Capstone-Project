# Script 1 — Valencia: Project Context

**Slides:** 1–7 (Title, Agenda, Project Context)
**Time:** about 3 minutes
**Your job:** open the defense and make the panel feel the bakery's problem before anyone shows them the solution. You set the story; everyone after you resolves it.

**Your flow:** Greeting → the industry is moving online → meet Pecto's Bakery → the problems it lives with every day → 10 steps that should be 5 → every problem has an answer → hand off to Yancy.

Cues: **[CLICK]** = next slide. **[PAUSE]** = stop for one breath. Words in **bold** = say them with weight.

### Slide 4 — The industry

> Let us start with the world outside the bakery.
>
> Small businesses everywhere are moving their orders online. And they are not doing it just to look modern. An order management system takes an order from the moment it is received until the moment it is delivered — with the **inventory**, the **payment**, and the **order status** all in one place.
>
> And the research tells us three things. [Point at each card.]
>
> **First — better inventory practice means a better business.** A review of ninety-five studies found that small businesses that manage their stock with modern practices and technology **run better**.
>
> **Second — reliable, trackable orders bring customers back.** In a study of over thirteen hundred food delivery customers, the ones who could rely on their order, and **track** it, were more satisfied — and more likely to **order again**.
>
> **Third — machine learning beat the old way of forecasting.** For a bakery chain, it predicted daily demand better than the traditional methods — especially on holidays and special days.
>
> [PAUSE] So the question is not *whether* small bakeries should go digital. The question is: **what happens to a bakery that hasn't?**

**[CLICK]**

### Slide 5 — Our client

> Meet Pecto's Bakery.
>
> Seventy-nine years in Lucban. **Five hundred to seven hundred customers every single day.** On weekends, they bake about **twice** as much. During Pahiyas, Holy Week, Christmas — about **four times** as much.
>
> And how are all those orders handled? [PAUSE] Through Facebook messages and the counter. **Written on paper. Passed from hand to hand.**
>
> We sat down with their General Manager, Mrs. Katherine Abillar, and she told us what that costs them.
>
> Orders given **verbally** come out wrong or incomplete. Large orders — over a hundred pieces — are the hardest to get right, and when they go wrong, someone has to face the customer and explain.
>
> Here is one she told us. An order is due at **six o'clock**. But the bread comes out of the oven at **seven** — and it still has to cool. [PAUSE] The customer waits. And because there is no way to check, the customer **messages the bakery to ask: "Where is my order?"**
>
> There are no customer records. And how much to bake tomorrow? That lives **in someone's memory.**

**[CLICK]**

### Slide 6 — 10 steps become 5

> Look at the left side. This is the life of one order today: **ten steps**, all by hand. Receive the message. Check the stock. Write it down. Confirm. Forward it. Prepare it. Count the stock again. Confirm the payment. Prepare the pickup. Update the customer.
>
> Ten chances to make a mistake.
>
> Now look at the right. With PECTRACK, it is **five**. The customer orders. The customer pays. And then — [point at step 3] — the system **records the order and deducts the stock by itself.** Then the bakery prepares it, and it is done.
>
> **That** is the heart of our study: the steps nobody should be doing by hand anymore, the system now does.

**[CLICK]**

### Slide 7 — Problem and solution

> So every problem we heard has an answer.
>
> **Miscommunication?** Every order goes into one list that all staff see.
> **Wrong orders?** Customers choose their own items, and every order gets a receipt.
> **Delays?** Orders come with a time slot, and the bakery sees what is needed next.
> **"Where is my order?"** The customer gets an email at every step.
> **No records?** Every customer has an account with their history.
> **Baking from memory?** The system makes the daily bake list.

> [Hand-off — turn toward Yancy]
>
> Those are the problems. **Yancy** will now show you the system we built to solve them.

## What to stress
- **Since 1947** and **500–700 customers a day.** These numbers make the panel care.
- On slide 4, read each card's **orange headline** first, then one line of what the study found. The sources are on the cards: Panigrahi et al. (2024), Wu et al. (2024), and Huber and Stuckenschmidt (2020).
- Connect the three findings to the bakery: **stock** (our automated inventory), **trackable orders** (our status emails), **forecasting** (our seven-day forecast).
- The **6:00 versus 7:00** story. It is real, it is from the interview, and it is easy to picture. Slow down on it.
- **"Ten chances to make a mistake."** Then the drop to five.

## Questions you might get
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Where did the 500–700 customers come from? | From our interview with the General Manager, Mrs. Katherine Abillar. |
| Are your sources local? | Labastida et al. (2024) is a Philippine study of a web-based ordering system with forecasting. Most local studies are in our Review of Related Literature. |
| Why is this "Order Management" and not "Order Processing"? | It covers the whole order: placing it, paying, stock, delivery and reports — not only recording the sale. |
